The garden scene

Many plants strewn over a plane at random ages. Three ideas carry it and there is not much else.

source: genetic_garden/garden.py · every figure on this page is drawn by the code it describes

1Depth does everything

Each plant gets a depth between 0 and 1. Depth sets how far down the frame it stands, how large it is, and how much haze sits over it. That is the entire perspective.

Sorting by baseline then gives the painter's order for nothing: near plants overlap far ones because they are drawn later.

2World scale, measured

Plants are drawn against shared world units rather than each fitted to its own box, so a mature tree towers over a mature flower.

Those units have to be measured from a built skeleton, not assumed from the stem length. Everything downstream of _trace — curvature, gravity, the crown — changes a plant's final height. The declared figures were originally guesses, and a test caught the consequence: mature flowers were being drawn taller than the shrubs behind them.

3Spacing, and plants that never grow

Placement is rejection sampling, but only against other plants at a similar depth — two plants far apart in the plane are meant to overlap, and rejecting that would flatten the scene.

In a crowded scene some plants never find room within their attempt budget and simply do not grow, which is why planted can come back lower than the count you asked for.

4Detail in a scene

Nothing in a garden earns full detail. Veins and per-leaf outlines are invisible at these sizes and a scene full of them runs to megabytes, so the auto ramp caps at compact and drops to icon detail below about 78 pixels tall.

A 26-plant scene lands around 400–500KB, which is fine for a page and worth rasterising if you are shipping many of them.
