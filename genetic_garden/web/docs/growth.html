Growth

Contributions become a maturity profile. One number drives everything, and it is never allowed to rearrange the plant.

source: genetic_garden/growth.py · every figure on this page is drawn by the code it describes

1One curve, and stages are bands on it

A project's count of sequential contributions maps onto a single number in [0, 1]. The mapping is logarithmic, so the first few contributions visibly move the needle and later ones refine — which matches how it feels to start something versus to keep it going.

The exponent on the end is doing real work. A plain logarithm puts the first contribution at 0.22, which draws a small adult plant rather than a sprout. Raising it to 1.6 pushes that back to 0.09 without flattening the rest of the curve.

2Five channels, not one

Maturity is read through a per-kingdom schedule into five separate channels, because the parts of a plant do not arrive together. Foliage comes in early; wood browns off in the middle; blossom is late, and much later on a tree than on a flower.

channelwhat it drives
scaleoverall size
depthhow many levels of branching
leafinesshow much foliage is out
bloomblossom openness and abundance
woodinessgreen stem → bark, and taper

3The constraint that shapes everything

the rule

The genome fixes the individual. Growth only extends it. A contribution may add structure, but it must never rearrange what was already there.

Break this and the garden stops working as a progress display: each contribution would redraw the plant as a different individual, and the eye would read change rather than growth.

Two things enforce it. The first is addressed randomness — every branch derives its RNG from its position in the tree rather than from a shared stream, so appending branches cannot disturb the ones already placed. The second is fractional depth.

4Fractional depth

If recursion depth were a whole number, a new layer of branches would pop into existence between one contribution and the next. Instead depth is fractional: the outermost level is scaled by the leftover fraction and grows out of its parent's tip.

5A bug this caused, and the fix

Fractional depth has one sharp edge, and it produced a genuinely bald tree. When a plant's depth lands just above a whole number — say 5.01 — the branch at remaining = 1.01 is not terminal, so it spawns children at remaining = 0.01. Those are below the minimum emergence and return immediately, drawing nothing and, crucially, registering no foliage tips.

The whole outer layer of the plant silently vanished, along with every canopy mass fitted to it. It only affected a narrow band of growth values, which is why it survived a lot of eyeballing.

# a branch whose children would all be too small to draw
# is itself the terminal one, and must carry the foliage
terminal = remaining - 1.0 <= MIN_EMERGENCE

6Fitting growth into a frame

How a plant is scaled into its frame turns out to matter as much as the growth model. Fit each stage to its own bounding box and every stage fills the frame — the growth signal is thrown away entirely.

The default instead scales against the plant's own mature size, so a seedling sits small in the corner and a mature plant fills the frame. A gamma under 1 compresses that range, because honest scaling leaves a sprout too small to recognise.
