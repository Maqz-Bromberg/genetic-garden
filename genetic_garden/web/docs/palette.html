Colour

Built in HSL from a few genome-driven anchors, so a plant's parts always harmonise — with one relationship that cannot be left to taste.

source: genetic_garden/palette.py · every figure on this page is drawn by the code it describes

1Anchors, not a palette

Each kingdom has a foliage hue, a bark hue and a bark lightness. Everything else is derived from those three by interpolation, so mutation shifts a plant's whole family of colours together instead of producing clashing parts.

Age is folded in at the same time: young foliage is yellower and lighter, and cools and deepens as the plant matures.

2The tone gap

the mistake worth avoiding

An early version had 0.026 luminance between a blade and the canopy mass under it. Every leaf was being generated, positioned, emitted — and then swallowed whole by the mass it lay on.

Foliage masses have to sit a clear step below the blades drawn over them. This is the one colour relationship in the system that is a correctness constraint rather than a preference: get it wrong and all the leaf geometry is wasted work that renders as a flat blob.

3Hue bands

Flowers do not come in cyan. A gene mapped onto the whole hue wheel produces cyan petals about a quarter of the time, and a garden of randomly seeded plants then looks like a colour picker rather than a garden.

So petal hues map into bands that real petals occupy — warm reds, yellows, pinks and purples — and berries into scarlet, orange, damson and sloe. Flower centres are always amber or brown, never a tint of the petal, because that contrast is what makes a bloom read at icon size.

4Nothing switches over

Every colour is a continuous interpolation on one of the growth channels. A stem is interpolated from green toward bark as woodiness climbs; a flower's never gets there.

There is no frame at which a plant changes species, which matters because consecutive contributions are meant to read as the same individual.

5Blossom on woody plants

Scattered blossom on a tree or a bush stays near white, cream and pink. Saturated primaries scattered over a crown read as fruit, not flowers — so the saturated end of the range is reserved for the cases that earn it: a flower's own bloom, and a hydrangea's trusses.

A tree can also go the other way entirely. In blossom mode the canopy masses themselves take the petal colour, which is what a crab apple looks like in April.
