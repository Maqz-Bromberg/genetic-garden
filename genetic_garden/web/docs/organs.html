Organs

Every blade in the system is one function with different constants. Blooms are the exception, and are sampled from a set of archetypes instead.

source: genetic_garden/organs.py · every figure on this page is drawn by the code it describes

1One function, every blade

Leaf, cotyledon, petal, sepal, bud, needle — all of them are the same parametric half-width profile. The exponent slides the widest point from the base to the tip, and a triangle wave serrates the margin.

This means the genome picks a shape out of a continuum rather than from a list, and there is exactly one piece of code to get right.

2Two curves, not sixteen

A blade sampled at a dozen points and run through a Catmull-Rom spline costs about 560 bytes. Fitting a single interpolating cubic through four points on the same margin costs about 90, and the error on a smooth curve is far inside a pixel.

Serrated margins still take the sampled path, because the teeth are the entire point of them — but they are only drawn at all when the leaf is more than about 24 pixels on screen, below which the teeth are invisible.

3Leaf architecture

A single blade is the common case, but a frond, a hand of leaflets or a needle tuft change a plant's texture more than any amount of recolouring. Which architectures a kingdom can grow differs: trees get compound and needle, bushes palmate and needle, flowers simple and palmate.

4Grain: bold few, or fine many

leaf_grain slides between a few bold leaves — with the canopy masses carrying the crown — and a haze of tiny ones that carries it itself, with the masses pulled back.

Fine grain costs more paths and simplifies worse at icon size. That trade is deliberately left exposed rather than decided in advance, because which end you want depends on whether you are drawing a hero image or a list bullet.

5Blooms are sampled, not scaled

A bell is not a wide daisy and a spike is not a small one. Trying to reach them by scaling a single rosette produces neither, so each archetype is its own construction and a gene picks between them.

Petal width falls as petal count rises, tied together so the petals and petal_width genes can vary freely without ever closing the whorl into a solid disc.

6The one organ that needs its own outline

The blade function pinches shut at both ends. That is exactly right for a leaf and a bud, and exactly wrong for a bell, which has to stay open at the mouth.

The first attempt used a blade with a high exponent and produced a flat magenta triangle. A bell needs an explicitly interpolated profile — a narrow neck at the calyx opening out to a rounded rim — which is why _cup exists and nothing else does.

def side(sign):
    pts = [put(u, sign * mouth * w)
           for u, w in ((0.0, 0.17), (1/3, 0.34),
                        (2/3, 0.62), (1.0, 1.0))]
    return cubic_through(*pts)

7Hydrangeas

A globe truss is drawn as a soft irregular dome with florets scattered over it, rather than as a hundred individual flowers — which would cost more than the rest of the plant put together. Its radius comes from the plant's own span, so a bush in full bloom really is about a third flower.

Two details matter. The edge is irregular and the florets are unoutlined: a clean circle full of outlined florets reads unmistakably as a blackberry. And globe-flowered bushes get saturated blue, pink and violet rather than the pale blossom used elsewhere, because near-white florets on a near-white truss have nothing to read against.
